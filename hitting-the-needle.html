Chapter 1: The Sounds of Science

“Now here we go dropping science, dropping it all over / Like bumping around the town, like when you're driving a Range Rover / Expanding the horizons and expanding the parameters / Expanding the rhymes of sucka MC amateurs” -Beastie Boys

Solomon Rosenthal did not seem like a high ranking member of a cult. He pays his taxes, goes to synagogue, and gives 110% when it comes to his driver's ed students. But on Saturday nights, he becomes King Solomon, holding dominion over Jinn.

King Solomon stands in the center of the twelve silent G.E.N.I members. They are seated in plastic folding chairs arranged in a dodecagram shape on the upper floor of Atomic Pizza. Like Solomon Rosenthal, Atomic Pizza does not seem like it has a connection to a cult. They serve mediocre pizza and have a 2.8 Yelp Score.

King Solomon rolls the 12-sided die. It hits the floor with a clatter. “Brother Michael, the die has decided. Disengage from your trauma,” says King Solomon in a paternal yet firm tone. King Solomon fiddles with his 1950s looking wristwatch.

Brother Michael gives a quick glance of fear to Sister Ashley, who sits across from him. Sister Ashley nods. He then proceeds to speak in a hushed shaky tone. “I don't know where to begin. I got into a nasty fight with my wife this week. It always starts with something small, like me not taking his shoes off when I enter the house. Then it escalates, she criticizes me for always working. I criticize her for being cold.”

Brother Michael continues for a while in his stream-of-consciousness. After 12 minutes and 12 seconds, King Solomon’s wristwatch goes off.

Brother Michael’s transformation is slow at first, then all at once. His eyes flash red. His speech slurs. He begins speaking a phrase in archaic Arabic that cannot be written down. The smell of mediocre pizza is replaced by noxious brimstone.

“Your wish, Brother Michael! What is your wish?!” shouts King Solomon through Brother Michael’s guttural utterances.

The last, fading spark of Brother Michael’s conscious mind screams a single thought: I wish my wife and I never argued. The moment the internal wish is cast, the guttural Arabic cuts out. The glowing red vanishes from his eyes, leaving a distraught, heavily sweating Michael collapsed in his folding chair.

Chapter 2: Genie in a Bottle

A weather worn pamphlet is taped haphazardly to a street light. The translucent tape over the words is grimy and obscuring. The paper underneath reads:

What does G.E.N.I stand for? Simple! It can be broken up into four fundamental forces!
G: Gravity
E: Electromagnetism
N: Nuclear
I: Interactions

Next to this explanation is a halftone image of a comforting and well groomed 1950s man, holding a pipe in his smiling mouth. He is surrounded by a concentric ring of protons and electrons.

Father Castellano walks by the streetlight, carrying a bag of groceries. He sneers when he sees the pamphlet. He brushes his cassock with his hand, as if the mere act of seeing it has made him dirty.

He enters the rectory for The Parish of St. Barbara the Martyr. He closes the door behind him gently.

He gets to the kitchen counter and begins taking out his boxes of spaghetti noodles and sauce. As he's about to turn on the stove, there is a frantic knocking at the door. Father Castellano adjusts his wire rim glasses and walks quickly towards the door.

“Can I help you?” he says as he delicately opens the door.

“Father, I need help. Bad.” says the gruff looking man wearing a zip-up Mets hoodie and a face of fear. He glances over his own shoulder nervously.

“Ah, Salvatore. Of course. I’ll take you to the confessionals.” says Father Castellano in a reassuring tone.
