The stars are beautiful, aren't they? Reminds me of sporadic development. Scattered buildings built haphazardly along roads with no grand plan behind them. People deciding to build houses wherever they please, wasting resources and creating inefficiency out of sheer convenience. Usually, the thought would bother me, but looking up at the stars, seeing how they're placed along the sky with no discernable pattern, I feel at peace. Some things just don't need to be planned. Marisa's dead body is right here. She's in algor mortis. I only have a little bit of time before it reaches rigor mortis. It wouldn't change anything, but that's when it will really hit me. I take a look at the entrenching tool resting in my survey kit. Funny. The only time any of it has actually come in handy. I grab the tool with my free hand. The other's still gripping tightly to my prismatic compass. Getting on my knees, I begin excavating the spoil. I'm not thinking of anything in particular. It's just mechanical. The soil is well graded. It's ironic. The clearing here, right next to the downstream river, secluded and quiet. It's the absolute perfect spot for a hydro-electric station. I'll never tell the COR or the Cardónians, of course. The hole looks big enough. I grab her body. It's not warm anymore. That's the algor mortis. With moderate effort, but major stress on my legs, I drop her into the hole. Getting on my knees again, I push the spoil over her with my tool. I take one last look at her face before covering it up. She still looks beautiful. I grab her copy of the Dictation from the ship. I don't know how to use this thing. I don't usually deal with poetry. Flipping through the pages, I think that there has to be some kind of funeral rite in here. My eyes glance over the page marked Requiescats. That's gotta be it. I flip through it until I found one I like, then clear my throat and begin to read aloud. > And it was so that like the Architect above, there is one of the deceased who his children loved. And like the Architect above we do not forget, we honor their life so their creation may be beget. I also take my flask out of my coat pocket and pour it on the made ground. I don't know why I do it, but I do. Then I take out my field book. 15 vi 27. Ontembaar, N. 118° to point of rocks. Ground unsuitable. I get back in the boat and leave. Following the stars for navigation. Despite their random placement, or maybe because of it, they still have use.
It all started in Krios of '27. That was when the COR sent me to Ontembaar. I got word from my superior Enguerrand Badeaux. He walked into my office with the stride of a man who felt he could reshape the world with his word alone. "Dabinett, have you been drinking?" he asked, the disgust in his tone palpable. I had. I felt no reason to lie to him. "Yes." Enguerrand's face didn't change. "I know. You're a slave to your impulses." This was his way of trying to reshape my behavior. He always thought appealing to the tradition of the Tanner thinker Glædwine was the best way to get through to me. It wasn't, and I could clearly see through it. "There's beauty in being a slave. You're fed, you have shelter. What's a little manual labor?" Clearly wanting to change the subject, Enguerrand proceeded. "They want you to go to Ontembaar. The Council is making a proclamation soon about the mandate." "Why do they want me?" I smiled. I just liked to hear him say it. Enguerrand's eyelid twitched. "They want you on the ground to plan the Cardónian city." "What about Henri? Surely the Cardónians want a city with some le montagnard influence?" Enguerrand was livid. "They want you." "Why?" Enguerrand was already at the door. "You're to be at the docks at 0500. Don't oversleep." I took a look at the map on my wall. Ontembaar is three weeks by ship from Linolia. I don't like ships. They're so unplanned. A hundred and ten people on average, all trapped together in a metal rookery. Nevertheless, they wanted me, like Enguerrand said. I could have never resisted the siren call of having tens of thousands all using my plans. A beautifully calibrated machine that could not exist without me. I began packing my tools.
